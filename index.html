GRAFT: Geometry-Aware Residual Adaptation for Foundation Trackers

Renjing Xu2, Mingliang Zhou1†, Diyun Xiang1

Abstract

Humanoid behavior foundation models (BFMs) provide reusable whole-body motion priors: given a kinematic reference, they generate coordinated joint commands for motion replay, teleoperation, and VLA control. However, trained primarily on flat-ground motion, BFMs lack an explicit mechanism to adapt foot placement, swing clearance, and contact timing to local terrain. Many terrain-aware BFM approaches address this limitation by retraining or fine-tuning the tracker together with perception, incurring substantial data and computation costs while coupling ordinary tracking to the perception pathway. To bridge this gap, we propose GRAFT, a geometry-aware residual adaptation framework for frozen behavior trackers. GRAFT preserves the BFM parameters and motion-reference interface, while a lightweight adapter adds terrain-conditioned corrections only to lower-body execution targets. The adapter is trained with task and foot-geometry rewards, regularized to suppress unnecessary corrections on flat ground, and distilled from privileged terrain observations to onboard depth. Because the frozen BFM already provides whole-body reference tracking and nominal locomotion, GRAFT reduces terrain learning to a focused execution-adaptation problem. We instantiate the framework on four frozen trackers and evaluate stairs, gaps, and platforms in simulation. We further validate it through real-world deployment on a Unitree G1, where the adapted SONIC tracker traverses terrain with onboard depth and unmodified references.

Method

GRAFT separates whole-body motion generation from terrain execution adaptation. The frozen BFM provides a reusable motion prior, including reference tracking, nominal locomotion, and whole-body coordination. A lightweight residual policy then learns the terrain-dependent deviation from this nominal execution.

Top: the frozen BFM maps the unmodified reference, from a recorded clip, a teleoperator or a VLA, to a nominal whole-body action, and the adapter reads onboard depth and adds a correction on the lower-body channels only. Bottom: the adapter is first trained against a privileged elevation map and then distilled into a student that reads onboard depth. The node marked gate closes the residual pathway at deployment, after which the executed target is the frozen BFM's own output. The adapter's proprioception, base-action and residual-history inputs are omitted for clarity.

Transfer Across Frozen Bases

Four frozen bases + GRAFT

The same adaptation procedure applies to four frozen bases (SONIC, Humanoid-GPT, HoloMotion and ScaleBFM). Each base receives a separate adapter with the same residual placement and objective family; only interface mappings and base-specific regularizers are adapted. Without GRAFT, the four bases obtain 3/40, 3/40, 8/40, and 2/40 successes, respectively. With GRAFT, these totals increase to 39/40, 34/40, 39/40, and 20/40.

Teleoperation Demo

Future work: teleoperation and VLA for mobile manipulation on complex terrain. We close with a teleoperation demo on the same reference interface: pick up an object, cross the stairs, and dispose of it. A next step is to evaluate tracking under live teleoperation and VLA commands, particularly when changes in upper-body motion require coordinated adjustments of balance and foot placement. Segments play at 3x, 1x, 10x and 3x speed.
